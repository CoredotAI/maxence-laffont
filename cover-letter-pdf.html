01
Where I come from

In 2022, I was accepted at both EFREI and ESTACA. After realising that aeronautics engineering — with its focus on physics and mechanics — wasn't for me, I joined Madeo, a small company specializing in cloud and IT infrastructure, where I handled server maintenance and on-site client support. It taught me what real infrastructure looks like in practice: hardware that fails, clients that need answers now, systems that have to stay up.

In 2023, I enrolled at IÉSEG — drawn by finance, entrepreneurship, and macroeconomics. Two years later, during my internship at Philippe Hottinguer Gestion (May–August 2025) — a Parisian wealth management firm — I worked as a private fund management intern. The teams were navigating increasing regulatory obligations (CSRD, SFDR) with extra-financial data scattered across PDFs, reports, and heterogeneous formats. I started building the first tools to assess what was technically feasible.

Strategic pivot — October 2025

During the same period — towards the end of my internship, from August 2025 — I started building a set of internal tools for Philippe Hottinguer Gestion to extract and consolidate extra-financial data from regulatory documents. The firm's teams were dealing with these obligations manually, and the data was scattered across PDFs and heterogeneous formats. When the Core.AI platform was suspended, these already-proven tools became the priority. From October 2025, I began incorporating them into a standalone platform: Core.AI ESG.

What I'm aiming for — and what I still need to learn

The platform exists, it works, and it's deployed. But the ESG data market is competitive and demanding — asset managers expect institutional-grade rigour, not just functional tools. To go from a working product to a defensible one, I need to strengthen what's already there: scoring models that hold up under audit, data pipelines built on formal methods, and the ability to engage technical and regulatory stakeholders on their own terms.

03
Why the SIMS–DDMI makes sense as a next step

In 2022, I was accepted at EFREI but chose a different path. Three years later, after IÉSEG, an internship, and a company, I'm coming back with a clearer picture of what I need. The SIMS curriculum addresses the scientific and technical foundations that would strengthen what I've already built, and the DDMI — if admitted after the SIMS — would let me earn both the IÉSEG Master in Management and the EFREI Engineering degree without leaving my current track.

The DDMI engineering internship is where this converges concretely: I can approach it with a project already underway, specific technical questions to solve, and an active institutional partner. I'm also applying to the IÉSEG incubator with Core.AI Systems — access to both entrepreneurial ecosystems supports what I'm building.

As a classmate, I'll be joining a class of business students stepping into a scientific and technical environment — just like me. What I can offer is simple: a genuine enthusiasm for the subjects we'll study, the habit of immediately trying to implement what I learn, and the willingness to exchange openly with teachers about how theory connects to practice. My side experiences may occasionally bring a different angle to group discussions — nothing more, but I hope it can be useful.
